# ADR-2169 — A drag is a hold: the released node rejoins the simulation and the graph answers the hand within a few ticks

## Context
- Owner requirement, 2026-11-01: up to two nodes held at once (one per controller), the whole graph responds as fast as practical on the server and every client, and a released node rejoins the physics instead of staying pinned.
- Before this change `nodeDragEnd` left the node pinned on the GPU until an explicit `nodeUnpin`. One global pin map served drags and pins alike, so a second client's release freed a node the first still held. A connection could hold five nodes.
- While a node was held nothing forced the GPU to step: the stability kernel and the orchestrator's equilibrium check could skip a still graph.
- The settings reheat adds uniform random velocity of up to twice its factor to every node on every step while it lasts (`inject_velocity_perturbation`, 0.997 decay per step to a 0.02 floor, about 1,300 steps from 1.0). Any write the GPU actor judged a change re-armed it. The orchestrator's FastSettle damping override and restore are such writes. The live log shows 22–1,680 consecutive reheat steps after each "params CHANGED", and the per-step "Reheating physics" line is that one reheat logged once per step, not a stream of senders.
- Measured step: about 9–10 Hz on the live graph (9,688 nodes), 84–94 ms of it the force kernel with the full live force set, on a card the backend shares with other work (it sits at 87–95 % utilisation). A ring of 9,478 nodes with only spring and repulsion steps in 4.5 ms on the same card, so the live cost is the force set and the sharing, not the node count; not investigated further. At the stored `globalSpeed` 0.4 a node held 300 units out moves its neighbours 4.5 units in 120 steps.

## Decision
1. **A drag is a hold, owned by a connection.** `nodeDragStart` and `nodeDragUpdate` send `HoldNodes{holder, holds}`; `nodeDragEnd`, the 2 s drag timeout and a disconnect send `ReleaseNodes` / `ReleaseHolder`. The node leaves the pinned set and rejoins the simulation. The last write wins when two connections hold one node, and a release counts only from the node's current owner. A connection holds at most two nodes (one per hand). A rate-limited update is coalesced and applied by a trailing timer or at drag end, not dropped.
2. **Explicit pins are separate.** `nodePin` (new, `{nodeId, position}`, ack `nodePinAck`) and `nodeUnpin` keep their meaning. A pin made before or during a hold survives the release; the node stays anchored where it was dropped.
3. **The GPU steps every tick while anything is held, by any client, and for 2 s after the last release.** The stability bypass is `warmup > 0 || any_held || settling`; the orchestrator never auto-pauses while a node is held and drops its 16 ms inter-step target (a no-op while a step takes longer than 16 ms, as it does on the live graph). A hold not written for 5 s is expired by the physics actor itself, and a disconnect always releases by holder, so a release that was lost or overtaken by its hold cannot leave the graph in hold mode.
4. **The settings reheat is withheld during a hold and the settle window**, and a grab clears any standing one. A damping-only `UpdateSimulationParams` is applied without re-arming the reheat or the 30 s warmup (the orchestrator's FastSettle override and restore were such writes). Pin events use a one-shot impulse (0.05) instead of a decaying reheat. The "params CHANGED" log now names the differing fields. **Not fixed:** the repeated settings PUTs from `dev-mode-local-admin` (about every 3 s) that the gate counts as changes; the next such PUT after the settle window closes re-arms the reheat at 1.0, and its per-step noise resumes until the field that differs is found and the cause removed.
5. **`global_speed` floors at 2.0 while held and through the settle window.** Under the live FA2 integrator velocity is set from `force * global_speed`, and the measured response (neighbour travel over 120 steps, ring graph) is 4.5 / 10.5 / 19 / 33 / 55 units at 0.4 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8. `damping`, `max_force` and `max_velocity` have no effect there. The settle-window damping cap was dropped on 2026-11-01 (owner); damping is always the stored value., so under FA2 it is inert.
6. **Amends ADR-2140 item 2 for the span of a hold. The owner accepted this on 2026-11-01 for holds and the 2 s settle window, after being shown R16's ring-graph measurement of about 22x more residual motion in the window, and dropped the settle-window damping cap (`damping` is always the stored value). To withdraw it, set `HOLD_GLOBAL_SPEED` to 0.0 in `force_compute_actor.rs`, which restores "applied exactly as stored".** ADR-2140 forbids any multiplier, clamp or floor between the store and the GPU. Items 3 and 5 are such an override: while a node is held or settling, the step runs with `global_speed = max(stored, 2.0)`; the stored document is untouched and the stored values apply again the moment the window closes. This is the same kind of transient override the FastSettle damping already is. If the owner wants the 2.0 to be stored, user-tunable values (compliant with ADR-2140 item 1), that is a schema, validator and client change and is open follow-up.
7. **Every client sees it.** The GPU's per-step snapshot is published to the client coordinator as before; nothing in the drag path broadcasts or paces. `/api/layout/status` now reports the GPU actor's iterations, settled flag and kinetic energy instead of fixed zeros.
8. **Amendment — spring mode while held (owner-accepted 2026-11-01).** The stored LinLog attraction (`log1p(dist) * weight`, ignores `springK`) and FA2 adaptive speed (velocity divided by `1 + sqrt(swing)`) left a held node's neighbours moving a median 0.35 units of a 183-unit drag on the live graph (0.19 %), so the owner saw no live spring motion. For a hold and through the 2 s settle window, and only then, the step runs an **elastic overlay**: the force pass adds `spring_k * (dist - rest) * edge_weight * spring_scale` along every edge, where `rest` is the edge length captured on the GPU (`snapshot_edge_rest_kernel`) when the overlay switched on, so the term is exactly zero at the moment of grab; and the integrator takes the Verlet path (`adaptive_speed` read as 0 for those steps, `prev_force` cleared so adaptive speed resumes with no stale swing). It is an overlay, not a model swap: LinLog and repulsion still run, `max_force` and `max_velocity` still clamp, no stored value is changed or retuned, and the resting layout style is unchanged. The snapshot is taken once per overlay activation: a second hold, a continuous hold, or a re-grab inside the settle window keeps it; a hold that begins after the window closed snapshots again. Plumbing: `StepOverrides.elastic_overlay` (same span as the bypass), `UnifiedGPUCompute::set_hold_overlay`, the `g_edge_rest_snapshot` device global (null = off; a global rather than a kernel parameter keeps `force_pass_kernel` at 30 parameters for Codescape, ADR-2143 I7, and `SimParams` and the feature word are untouched). This is a further ADR-2140 item 2 exception, active only while held and settling; withdraw it by returning `false` from `elastic_overlay` in `step_overrides`. **Measured behaviour the owner should know:** (a) the overlay is a rubber band: a released node is pulled back toward its pre-grab rest lengths while the window is open (dense fixture: hub released at +60 returned to +21.6 after 120 overlay steps), so a drag does not place a node, the layout forces and the settle do; (b) the response is bounded by the step rate and by degree, a neighbour with about 30 other springs settles near `1/(1+30)` of the hub travel; (c) any held-only multiplier on `springK` is **an owner decision and is not implemented**. **Interaction with items 5 and 6:** the overlay's span is exactly the `HOLD_GLOBAL_SPEED` span and the Verlet integrator ignores `global_speed`, so while the overlay is on the 2.0 floor of items 5 and 6 has no effect, and item 6's withdrawal recipe (`HOLD_GLOBAL_SPEED = 0.0`) changes nothing. Withdrawing this item (`elastic_overlay` returning `false`) brings the FA2 behaviour and the floor back. The constant is left in place; removing an owner-accepted override is the owner's call. **Superseded in part by item 9:** the release now places the node (no pull-back) and a held-only gain exists.


9. **Amendment — held-only spring gain, force cap scaled with it, and a release that places the node (owner-decided 2026-11-01: "both raise stored k and multiply, we need a big effect"; "place it").** Three changes to item 8, all active only while a node is held (or, for the Verlet integrator, settling). (i) **Gain:** while any node is held, `execute_physics_step_with_bypass` multiplies `spring_k` by `HOLD_SPRING_GAIN` (`src/utils/unified_gpu_compute/mod.rs`, **20**). Under the stored LinLog mode `spring_k` is read only by the overlay term, so this is a gain on the overlay alone. (ii) **Force cap:** `max_force` is multiplied by the same gain for the same span, because the stored 150 saturates a `springK` 12 spring at 12.5 units of stretch and the gain would otherwise change nothing. `max_force` is a coefficient as well as a ceiling: the hard overlap push is `max_force * penetration` in both force kernels and the boundary force is `max_force` itself, so while held every overlapping pair and every node past the boundary is pushed 20 times harder, not only allowed to be. In Hooke mode (`lin_log_mode` off) the stored edge springs read `spring_k` as well, so they are 20 times stronger too. The `max_force` and `spring_k` figures in this item therefore describe a gain on those terms; they are not only a raised cap on the overlay. It is a further per-hold ADR-2140 item 2 exception, alongside the `global_speed` floor (item 6, inert under Verlet) and the Verlet span (item 8); the stored document is untouched and the gain is not applied once the hold ends. **Open conflict between two owner decisions, for the lead:** the cap scaling was asked for "while held or settling", and the release was asked to place the node. This change scales the cap only while the springs run (a hold). Scaling it through the settle window as well lifts the cap on LinLog attraction for a heavy node: a 1,200-neighbour hub is then pulled 26.5 units back toward the grab point in the 2 s window, against 1.55 with the cap at its stored value. The switch is one line (`springs_active()` to `is_active()` on the `max_force` multiplier in `execute_physics_step_with_bypass`). (iii) **Place it:** the springs end at release. `StepOverrides.hold_springs = any_held` drives `set_hold_springs`; `elastic_overlay` (the Verlet integrator, `adaptive_speed` read as 0, `prev_force` cleared) still spans the hold and the 2 s settle window, so the release has the same stability as before, but no term pulls toward the pre-grab rest lengths. Every spring off to on transition takes a fresh snapshot, so a re-grab inside the window measures the shape the node was dropped into (this replaces the "re-grab keeps the snapshot" rule of item 8). **Recommended stored value: `springK` 30** (from 12), written through `PUT /api/settings/physics`; `maxForce` stays 150 (the gain scales it while held) and the stored `maxVelocity` 100 is not changed. **Measured (probe shape: hub of 1,200 neighbours with 17 leaves, weight 1.1, mass 2.7, 183-unit drag over 30 steps and 20 held steps, RTX 6000 Ada, GPU 1):** one-hop median follow 0.026 at gain 1, 0.336 at gain 20 with `springK` 30 (two hops 0.189); the maximum node speed reaches `maxVelocity` 100, which is the limit: gain 40 adds only 0.03, and a 300-unit drag reaches only 0.22 because 50 steps at `maxVelocity` x `dt` cannot carry the neighbours farther. No NaN, and the tail speed of the settle window is about 0.23 of the peak. Hop-3 nodes end a median 18 units from their pre-grab place after release and settle, against 11.4 for a run with no drag. **What "placed" means, measured:** the snapshot spring is a small effect in a 2 s window at the live step rate, because Verlet integrates `position += velocity * dt` and the force cap bounds the speed. On a low-degree held node (a leaf of the probe graph, 183-unit drag, 100-step window, no gain) the springs of `1a16d43b3` kept through the window pull it back 11.6 units, and with the springs ended at release it moves 1.5 (the GPU test `a_released_leaf_is_placed_where_it_was_dropped`, which fails with the springs kept). For a 1,200-neighbour hub the snapshot makes no measurable difference in the live 2 s window: the hub moves 1.55 units back toward the grab point whether the springs are kept at gain 1 or dropped (the cap is 150 in both), and 26.5 whenever the cap is multiplied by 20 through the window, with the springs kept or dropped (measured both ways, the second with a temporary change that is reverted). After the window the stored LinLog and the 1,200 neighbours, which followed a median 34 %, pull a hub that heavy back towards its old place (after 400 further steps it was 14 units from where it started); that is the layout, not the overlay, and only a pin or a larger follow keeps such a hub where it was dropped. The 2.0 `global_speed` floor remains inert under Verlet and is left for the owner. Withdraw by returning `false` from `elastic_overlay` (item 8) or by setting `HOLD_SPRING_GAIN` to 1.0 (item 9 (i) and (ii) only). The live effect is not established: the fixture is synthetic and the lead has not run the headset probe.

10. **Constraints are not part of the hold (owner, 2026-11-01).** The ontology constraints act at their stored strength through a hold, the 2 s settle window and warm-up, as they do at idle; none of the hold overrides (items 5 to 9) touches them. The actor uploads the constraint set when it changes and not on every step, which is what made the constraints exert no force on bypass steps. ADR-2173 records the mechanism and the evidence.

## Consequences
- A client that relied on the drag-end pin must send `nodePin`. The XR client's own pin set (`fx/fy/fz`) is a client follow-up (ADR-2172 stops recording drag releases as pins).
- Time to rest after a release is bounded by the FA2 integrator, not by damping. At `globalSpeed` 2.0 the neighbourhood moves about four times faster, but the ring measurements show almost no decay of residual energy within 40 steps at any `globalSpeed`, so **rest within about 2 s of a release is not established**; the live graph has to be measured (`scripts/drag-physics-acceptance.mjs`).
- The tick rate stays at the GPU-bound 9–10 Hz on the live graph; nothing here raises it. ADR-2173 later found and removed the dominant cost (the force kernels scanned every ontology constraint per node); on a graph of the live shape the step falls from 29 to 9.8 ms, to be confirmed on the live card after redeploy. Per-client delivery is ack-paced (ADR-2141) and was not measured.
- Known gap: the orchestrator tracks held nodes by node id only, and a release is forwarded to it even when the physics actor ignored it (another connection owns the node). Two connections holding one node can therefore make the orchestrator think nothing is held; the live effect is nil today (`pauseOnEquilibrium` is false, the interval change is a no-op).
- Why the live settings PUTs (about every 3 s, `dev-mode-local-admin`) register as changes while the visible fields are equal is not established; the gate returns "unchanged" for the live document in a unit test, so a hidden field differs between PUTs.
